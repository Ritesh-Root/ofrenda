Rosa Elena Méndez Cruz

1938 2019

Barrio de Jalatlaco, Oaxaca

Her ofrenda: eight stories from four of her family, kept on their own laptop. Ask it anything. It answers only with what they remembered.

Who she was

She sold chocolate from the same stall for forty-three years, walked slowly on purpose, and grew marigolds in paint buckets when she could no longer get to the market.

Her stories, as they were told

Lupita, her daughter

Mole on Sundays 1974

Every Sunday my mother started the mole negro before the church bells at Santo Domingo rang for seven o'clock mass. She toasted the chilhuacles until the whole street coughed. Nobody was allowed to stir the pot but her. When I was fourteen she finally handed me the wooden spoon and said, “Now you will never be hungry, and neither will anyone you love.”

Also told by Lupita: The road to Monte Albán

Tío Chucho, her brother

El puesto de chocolate 1961

Rosa abrió su puesto en el Mercado 20 de Noviembre con un metate prestado y tres kilos de cacao. Molía el chocolate con canela y almendra delante de los clientes, porque decía que la gente compra lo que ve hacer. Yo le cargaba los costales y ella me pagaba con pan de yema.

Also told by Chucho: Hay más tiempo que vida

Mateo, her grandson

The song she sang off-key 2006

Abuela sang “Canción Mixteca” while she washed dishes, always a little flat on the high part, and she knew it. If you laughed she sang louder. It was the song her father whistled walking down from the Sierra to sell coffee. I didn't understand why it made her quiet until I caught myself humming it on a bus in Monterrey.

Also told by Mateo: Pan de muerto for the dog

Ana Sofía, her granddaughter

Marigolds from the roof 2018

Her last October she couldn't go to the market, so she grew cempasúchil in paint buckets on the roof. She made me lay a line of petals from the front door to the ofrenda, and then she checked it and fixed the corners. “If the path is crooked they get lost,” she said. I lay it straight every year.

Also told by Ana: Al mal tiempo, buena cara
